Architecture research · Phase 1 · October 2026

Vault

A computer that only its owner, and the successors they name, can use. It verifies who is at the keyboard continuously and handles coercion without endangering the operator. Built first for the people who hold the keys to frontier AI models.

Bottom line

Vault should build the secretless terminal first.

Vault should build first a hybrid architecture: a dedicated terminal that never holds model weights or long-lived secrets, a secure element cryptographically paired to the terminal's sensors that matches the operator's biometric on-device and signs a steady stream of short-lived presence attestations, and a lab-controlled verifier that keeps a seconds-scale session key alive inside the confidential-computing environment holding the weights only while those attestations keep arriving. Any irreversible, high-impact action (bulk export, a new egress destination, a change to the quorum or the heir list) additionally needs a biometric-bound K-of-N quorum and a time delay. The case rests on a specific gap. Frontier labs' published controls verify possession of a token and approval by a second person, but none publicly verifies that the enrolled human is the one at the terminal for the whole session, or that the human is acting freely, even though RAND scores bribery and extortion of insiders as 60–80% likely to succeed against organized-crime and standard state attackers[1, 2, 3]. No single architecture closes that gap. Software agents share a privilege domain with the attacker, hardware biometrics authenticate only at a point in time, confidential computing protects weights but has no notion of a human, and keys derived from biometrics top out around 72–84 bits[4, 5, 6, 7]. Duress should be handled the way vault locks, pharmacies and hardware wallets handle it: an explicit covert signal, silent and indistinguishable degradation, and quorum plus delay so that no single coerced person can deliver the payoff. Stress inference is the wrong tool; it fails statistically, and for employees in the EU it is presumptively prohibited under AI Act Art. 5(1)(f) when the purpose is protecting property[8, 9, 10]. Succession, whether to a founder's heirs or to a lab's successor operators, is a key-ceremony problem (owner-signed delegation, quorum, veto windows and a non-biometric break-glass path), never a biological one. The buyer set is small, an estimated 5–12 frontier developers plus a ring of infrastructure and government holders, but no shipping product found in this research combines continuous hardware-attested human binding, co-presence, duress handling and weight-egress integration.

Labelling conventions used throughout: "vendor claim" marks a figure published by a vendor and not independently evaluated; "secondary" marks news, aggregator or search-summary coverage that was not checked against the primary source; "proposal" or "inference" marks design reasoning produced by this research rather than established fact.

01 · Executive summary

Labs verify tokens, Vault must verify people

Making a computer usable by exactly one person, and later by the successors that person names, breaks into four problems that the evidence treats very differently. Binding (is this the enrolled human?) is well served by mature hardware: secure elements that release a key only after an on-device match. Continuity (is it still the same human minutes later?) has strong components but no shipping product that signs a continuous human-presence stream into the system holding the protected asset. Freedom (is the human acting voluntarily?) cannot be measured reliably, so it has to be designed around rather than detected. Succession (who may act next?) is a cryptographic-governance problem with well-tested patterns from wallets, password managers and HSM key ceremonies. Vault's thesis follows from that split: build binding and continuity in hardware, keep the weights off the terminal entirely, make every irreversible action need several live, verified people plus a delay, give operators a covert way to signal duress, and treat succession as a quorum ceremony. Frontier AI labs are the right first market because their own reference threat model, RAND's Securing AI Model Weights, puts insiders and extortion at the center and asks for these properties at SL3–SL5[1].

Seven findings support the thesis:

  1. The gap is human binding, continuity and freedom, not cryptography. Lab frameworks name MFA, hardware-backed authentication, managed devices and two-party approval[2, 3, 11]. None publicly commits to biometric-bound or continuously verified operator identity, or to duress controls. Meanwhile RAND scores "bribes and cooperation" and "extortion" at 4 on its 5-point scale (60–80% chance of success) for organized-crime and standard state attackers[1]. This research found no shipping product that combines continuous, hardware-attested human presence with confidential-computing session teardown, co-presence and duress handling. That is absence of evidence from a search, not proof.
  2. No single architecture is enough; a specific hybrid is. Software agents share the attacker's privilege domain; SentinelOne's incident data show security tools disabled in about 48% of 2024 intrusions[4]. Hardware biometrics gate key use, but only at a moment[5, 12]. Confidential computing protects weights from administrators but knows nothing about humans[6, 13]. Biometric-derived keys reach only about 72 bits (fingerprint) and 84 bits (face)[7]. HSM and MPC quorums bind shares to cards and PINs, not to people[14, 15]. The recommended hybrid combines a secretless terminal (A3), sensor-paired secure-element matching (A2) and biometric-bound quorum (A5). Software telemetry (A1) may only reduce privilege, and biometric cryptosystems (A4) are confined to template privacy.
  3. 3D face on an authenticated sensor path is the only accurate, passive continuity signal. NIST face evaluations show the matcher is not the bottleneck: leading 1:1 algorithms reach roughly 0.14–0.16% false non-match on cooperative border and visa images, per a trade-press summary[16]. Capture conditions and spoofing are the bottleneck. Vein, iris and fingerprint suit explicit step-up checks. Behavioral signals need about 18 seconds to several minutes, or hundreds of actions, to flag an impostor[17, 18]. NIST SP 800-63B-4 bars voice outright[19].
  4. Injection, not masks, is the attack to engineer against. Vendor threat data report native virtual-camera attacks up 2,665% in 2024[20]. FIDO's v4.1 biometric certification sets no numeric threshold for injection[21], and the first injection-detection standard, CEN/TS 18099, is only provisional[22]. The dependable defense is architectural: a firmware-locked sensor whose output is authenticated to the secure element, as Apple and Windows ESS already do[23, 12].
  5. Duress must be signaled, not inferred, and coercion must be made unprofitable. The benchmark lab stress classifier reaches 93% binary accuracy on 15 subjects[24]. Field performance drops sharply[25, 26], no dataset covers real coercion, and base rates guarantee that inferred alarms are mostly false[27, 28]. The battle-tested pattern is explicit covert signals (duress PINs, fingers, codes), silent and indistinguishable degradation, two-person rules and time delays[9, 29, 30].
  6. Law points to the same design. EU AI Act Art. 5(1)(f) presumptively prohibits inferring employees' stress or anxiety from biometric signals. The Commission reads the safety exception as covering life and health, "not … property against theft or fraud"[10]. On-device 1:1 verification is the best-supported position under GDPR and the AI Act[31, 32, 10], and it limits, but does not eliminate, BIPA exposure[33].
  7. Succession is a key ceremony, never biology. Every shipping inheritance product pairs cryptography with a waiting period and a human or legal attestation[34, 35, 36]. DNA is slow, not secret, unreliable for half-siblings and legally toxic for US employers[37, 38, 39, 40]. In a lab, "heirs" are successor operators under M-of-N custodianship, with a non-biometric break-glass path[41, 42].
02 · Threat model

Six adversaries converge on the authenticated session

RAND's framework is the reference threat model for weight security, and it defines each security level by the attacker it defeats. SL3 must stop "cybercrime syndicates or insider threats (OC3)", SL4 "most standard operations by leading cyber-capable institutions", and SL5 "most top-priority operations by the top cyber-capable institutions"[1]. Its catalogue of about 38 attack vectors includes compromising existing credentials, undermining the access-control system itself, falling back to "alternative (less secure) authentication or access schemes", evading physical access control, and the human-intelligence vectors of bribery, extortion and candidate placement[1]. The six threat classes below regroup those vectors around the one asset Vault controls: the authenticated session.

Insiders. RAND groups insiders with crime syndicates "because the level of investment required to robustly defend against them" is comparable. It says a key goal of SL3 is reducing insider risk, which "simultaneously reduces the risk from attackers who can masquerade as insiders"[1]. Labs acknowledge the gap. Anthropic amended its ASL-3 standard in May 2025 to exclude "both sophisticated insiders and state-compromised insiders"[43]. Its RSP v3.0 sets an aspiration, at automated-AI-R&D capability, of controls on employees "up to and including the company's CEO"[44]. The incident record is insider-heavy. Former Google engineer Linwei Ding was convicted in January 2026 on seven counts of economic espionage and seven of theft of trade secrets, after uploading more than two thousand pages of AI-infrastructure secrets to a personal cloud account over about a year[45]. News coverage of the indictment reported an allegation that a colleague repeatedly scanned Ding's badge while he was in China[46]. Meta's LLaMA weights, distributed to vetted researchers, appeared on 4chan and BitTorrent within days in March 2023[47]. Ding took design documents, not weights, but the failure mode generalizes: presence was proven by possession (a badge), possession was delegated, and nothing verified that the credential holder was the person acting.

Coercion (the "$5 wrench"). RAND describes extortion that threatens "physical harm, legal harm, financial harm" and notes that "leverage can also be applied to friends and family of the target". It scores extortion 4 of 5 for OC3 and OC4 attackers and 5 for OC5[1]. Yet its only SL5 countermeasure is "proactive protection" of staff through guards and counterintelligence[1]; the reference framework contains no technical duress control. The best public quantitative proxy comes from a different population. Jameson Lopp's catalogue of about 225 verified physical attacks on cryptocurrency holders (a conference-talk transcript, secondary) finds about 66% succeed, nearly 25% are home invasions and about 23% are kidnappings[48]. Irish "tiger kidnappings", in which bank staff or their families were held hostage to force a staff member to remove cash, show the hardest variant: the operator arrives alone, calm-looking and fully authenticated. The parliamentary record of that response protocol could only be partially verified[49]. No source quantifies how often AI-lab operators are coerced.

Session hijack after login. Most access systems authenticate once and then trust the session. Microsoft Entra Continuous Access Evaluation propagates critical revocation events with latency "up to 15 minutes" and issues session tokens valid for up to 28 hours[50]. Teleport's documentation concedes that per-session MFA "does not block a compromised credential from running other actions"[51]. NIST SP 800-63B-4 caps AAL3 sessions at 12 hours, with a recommended 15-minute inactivity timeout, and does not describe continuous biometric authentication at all[19]. RAND asks for more as early as SL2: FIDO authentication "either for every interaction or for any action that is attempted after a short period (e.g., 60 seconds)"[1]. Bojinov et al. add the coercion twist: with remote authentication, "an attacker could coerce a trained user to authenticate to a remote server and then hijack the session"[52].

Deepfake and injection presentation attacks. CEN/TS 18099 separates injection from presentation attacks on the sensor. Injection means feeding synthetic or replayed data into the pipeline through virtual cameras, emulators, hooked functions or manipulated traffic[22]. A vendor certified for ISO/IEC 30107-3 presentation-attack detection (PAD) may still be vulnerable to injection, according to a vendor blog[53]. iProov's threat report, a vendor source, counts native virtual-camera attacks up 2,665% and face swaps up 300% in 2024[20]. Real-world deepfakes already move money: engineering firm Arup reportedly lost about US$25M after an employee joined a video call in which every "colleague" was synthetic (news coverage)[54]. Local hardware injection is proven too. CyberArk's custom USB device fed Windows Hello a single valid IR frame and unlocked the PC (CVE-2021-34466)[55].

Lost or stolen device and physical tampering. Blackwing Intelligence, commissioned by Microsoft, found three "100 percent reliable" bypasses of match-on-chip fingerprint sensors on Dell, Lenovo and Surface hardware. Each needed physical access and a small man-in-the-middle device, and vendors have since patched them[56]. On the server side, TEE.fail (disclosed October 2025, IEEE S&P '26) used a DDR5 bus interposer that "should cost you under $1000". It extracted Intel's provisioning certification key, forged TDX attestations and "borrowed" NVIDIA GPU attestations. Intel and AMD treat physical interposers as out of scope[57, 58]. Apple's Stolen Device Protection shows the consumer mitigation pattern for changing recovery contacts or enrolled biometrics away from familiar locations: biometric, a one-hour security delay, then biometric again[59].

OS and administrator compromise. SentinelOne's incident data show security tools disabled in about 48% of 2024 intrusions, and "EDR killers" are now a maintained capability bundled into ransomware-as-a-service[4]. Singapore's CSA documented an EDR killer that loads a signed vulnerable driver to blind endpoint protection[60]. At Black Hat 2025, ERNW showed that a local administrator could insert biometric data into the Windows Hello database, which is protected only by CryptProtectData, so that any face unlocked the victim's PC. The hypervisor-isolated Enhanced Sign-in Security (ESS) mode blocks the attack, but not all hardware supports it[61].

Every class attacks either the moment after authentication or the path between a sensor and the decision it feeds. That yields four design principles applied in the rest of this report: keep secrets off the endpoint, authenticate the sensor path, fail closed when evidence stops arriving, and require more than one live human for anything irreversible. The geopolitical temperature is rising. News coverage of a July 2026 House Intelligence hearing reported witnesses saying China's MSS "now looks at frontier model companies"[62]. In October 2026, per news coverage, Proofpoint attributed to the China-aligned group TA419 a campaign that impersonated a senior Anthropic employee[63].

03 · Architectures

Each fails alone, together they cover SL3–SL4

A1. A software agent on a commodity OS is a sensor, not a lock

Continuous webcam and behavioral authentication delivered as an agent on macOS, Windows or Linux already ships. SecureAuth SessionGuardian runs "periodic facial checks" across browser, desktop and VDI sessions, and locks on "additional or unknown faces in the frame". Its documentation (vendor) does not state the check interval, where matching happens, or any liveness or injection defenses[64]. OLOID markets presence detection that locks devices when users step away (vendor)[65]. This research found no independent evaluation of either. The structural problem is that attacker and enforcer share a privilege domain: whoever holds admin or kernel rights can kill the agent, feed it injected frames, or tamper with its template store, as the EDR-killer and Windows Hello results above show[4, 61]. A commodity webcam has no authenticated path to the matcher. FIDO's biometric certification only asks vendors to document injection countermeasures, which the lab then checks; it sets no measured threshold[21]. NIST SP 800-63B-4 requires that the biometric "be presented and compared for each authentication operation" and says verifiers SHOULD check sensor integrity, for example through signed attestation[19]. The research notes conclude (inference) that A1 belongs in Vault only as telemetry: fast absence detection, a second-face alarm and behavioral anomaly scoring. Its output may only reduce privilege, and the server must treat a missing heartbeat as revocation so that killing the agent fails closed.

A2. Hardware-rooted matching gates keys, but only at a moment

The key distinction is whether a match releases a key inside a secure element or merely returns a boolean to the OS. Apple's Touch ID sensor talks to the Secure Enclave over a channel encrypted and authenticated with AES-CCM using a per-sensor, factory-provisioned key, so the application processor forwards data it "can't read". Face ID capture data are "digitally signed and sent to the Secure Enclave"[23]. On a match, the biometric subsystem "provides the key for unwrapping the Data Protection keys". Keychain items can be released "only by a successful match", and app-created Secure Enclave keys are used only inside the enclave "after it authorizes their use". The plain app API, by contrast, only reports "whether the authentication was successful"[5]. Windows ESS runs the face matcher under virtualization-based security, lets only the face camera write into hypervisor-protected memory, requires match-on-sensor fingerprint readers with a Microsoft certificate burned in at manufacture, and uses a boot-time secure channel to the TPM to "authorize the usage of Windows Hello keys". ESS face does not support external cameras, and on multi-user devices "the lowest Windows Hello security posture applies"[12]. Android's Class 3 biometrics can gate per-operation Keystore keys while Class 2 cannot[66]. YubiKey Bio stores and matches fingerprints on a separate secure element (vendor claim)[67]. Apple claims a random-person false-match rate below 1 in 1,000,000 for Face ID and below 1 in 50,000 for Touch ID (vendor claim)[68]. FIDO's lab-measured BioLevel 2 bound is 1:10,000, which is the same as NIST's floor[21, 19]. The weakness is time: every shipping design matches on an explicit event and then hands an unlocked session to a general-purpose OS, and this research found no public API for continuous Secure Enclave face re-verification on macOS. The strongest pattern, per the notes' inference, is a non-exportable signing key inside the secure element whose every use requires a fresh match. Each presence attestation in A3 would be signed that way.

A3. A secretless terminal plus confidential computing has a server half but no human half

The server half ships today. NVIDIA H100 confidential-computing mode is attested through NVIDIA's Remote Attestation Service, which returns a JWT[69]. GPU confidential computing relies on a CPU confidential VM (Intel TDX or AMD SEV-SNP)[70]. Azure confidential VMs with H100 reached general availability in September 2024[71], and Google Cloud's A3 confidential VMs with H100 are generally available[72]. An independent benchmark found low overhead for LLM inference that shrinks with model size, near zero at 70B parameters[73]. Anthropic and Pattern Labs (now Irregular) published a design (vendor research) in which weights are "stored encrypted, decrypted at the loader, and never released from there", and a keyserver releases decryption keys only to an environment whose attestation proves isolation, signed code and disabled debugging[6]. Fortanix sells a product that releases weights only after composite CPU+GPU attestation (vendor claim)[74]. IETF RATS (RFC 9334) supplies the vocabulary: Attester, Verifier, Relying Party, freshness through nonces, timestamps or epoch IDs, and a Hardware Watchdog use case in which a device lacking "regular and fresh Attestation Results" is forced to reboot. RFC 9334 does not address attesting humans[13].

The human half does not exist as a product. The closest analogs are event-driven revocation (Entra CAE)[50], per-session MFA and live moderated sessions in Teleport[75], periodic face-check agents[64], research on TEE-based "process attestation" of continuous human activity[76], and a 2019 DISA pilot of chipsets that continuously verified users by gait, face, voice and location, whose production status is unknown[77]. The notes infer that Vault maps cleanly onto RATS. The terminal's secure element is the Attester, emitting evidence that combines a fresh match result, the measured boot state and the current epoch. A lab-controlled Verifier appraises that evidence, and the confidential VM or the keyserver is the Relying Party, which drops the session key when no fresh result arrives within T seconds. Residual risks remain. The terminal still displays outputs. The presence signal is only as strong as the A2 sensor path. TEE.fail shows server attestations can be forged with physical access, and NVIDIA GPU reports are not bound to a specific CVM, so the Verifier must appraise CPU and GPU evidence as one bound composite and facility controls stay in the trusted base[57].

A4. Biometric cryptosystems are a privacy layer, not a root of trust

Fuzzy extractors, fuzzy vaults and related schemes try to derive a stable key from a noisy biometric. The September 2026 state of the art ("Sample-then-Correct") says existing constructions "do not provide strong security for the biometric modalities deployed on modern devices". It improves them to about 72 bits (fingerprint), 84 bits (face) and 111 bits (iris), at a true-accept rate of roughly 90% over multiple readings and about 60% from a single reading[7]. Information-theoretic analysis suggests that at realistic error rates sketches may leak most of a biometric's entropy[78]. Fuzzy-vault correlation attacks link a person across databases[79]. What has been deployed is secure multiparty matching, not key derivation. World moved iris-code uniqueness checks to SMPC in May 2024 and deleted its old iris codes[80], and Anonybit shards templates across nodes (vendor claim)[81]. The notes conclude (inference) that Vault must never derive a weight key from a biometric. A4 is useful only for privacy-preserving re-verification and cancelable templates, for example during heir enrollment or recovery.

A5. Threshold control and two-person integrity are mature but bound to cards

M-of-N quorum is standard in HSMs. Thales Luna splits authentication secrets across up to 16 PED keys[14], and Entrust nShield requires K of N administrator cards assigned to different role holders[15]. In MPC custody, Fireblocks says the private key is never assembled and that policy is enforced inside Intel SGX (vendor claim)[82], an enclave family that physical interposer attacks have since broken[57]. Two-person integrity has a formal definition: a system that prevents lone access by requiring at least two authorized people, each able to detect improper procedures[83]. Teleport offers software analogs in moderated sessions and dual authorization[75]. G42's frontier safety framework cites "multi-party and quorum-based approval for high-sensitivity operations" for weights, as quoted in an academic analysis[84]. The gap is that no shipping product found here binds each quorum share to a per-member biometric match; shares follow possession and PINs. The notes propose (inference) shares that release only on a fresh on-device match, which turns "K of N cards" into "K of N specific people". Because an MPC round per keystroke is impractical, quorum should open a time-limited session key and continuous presence should keep it alive.

Real high-security deployments today mostly combine physical access control, smartcards, session timeouts and static biometrics. FDA rules for electronic signatures already rely on "a single, continuous period of controlled system access"[87]. Continuous biometric authentication remains pilot-grade, which is both Vault's opening and its adoption risk. For a founder's personal Vault used at home, FIPS terminology calls that a "physically unprotected environment"[88]. The notes argue (inference) for stricter server-side policy there (shorter attestation lifetimes, geofencing, an extra quorum member) rather than trying to turn a laptop-class device into a Level 4 HSM.

Two conclusions follow. First, the matcher is not where the risk lies. Face matching on cooperative images has converged at very low error, and the weak points are capture conditions for a seated, moving, partly occluded operator, demographic differentials, and spoofing and injection[16, 90]. A 3D/IR sensor on a hardware-authenticated path matters more than which matcher is chosen. Second, behavioral signals are too slow to be primary. At typical typing rates, windows of roughly 94–275 actions leave an impostor minutes of keyboard time before a behavioral-only lockout[18, 107]. That is unacceptable as the sole control on weight exfiltration but useful for nudging a trust score.

The literature on continuous authentication converges on score-level fusion[111] and on a trust variable that each observation raises or lowers, with lockout below a threshold and, more recently, "temporal trust decay"[90]. DARPA's Active Authentication program set the usability reference: the system may not falsely reject the user "more than five times in a row during continuous usage over a 40 hour period"[17, 112]. The notes propose a Vault trust model (proposal). A strong explicit check (vein, iris or fingerprint plus a hardware key) sets trust T to 1. T decays with a half-life of minutes. Each passive 3D-face match adds a bounded increment, a face mismatch causes a large step down, and keystroke and mouse adjust T with small weights. Privileged actions require T above a high fixed threshold, which forces a step-up when trust is stale; the fixed threshold matches NIST's requirement at the decision point[19].

The hardest operational distinction is "went to get coffee" versus "impostor swapped in." Presence sensing is not identity verification. Windows presence sensing can lock on leave and wake on approach[113], but an impostor who sits down inside the lock timeout defeats presence alone. Proposed policy (inference): lock the screen within seconds of face absence. Keep the session key alive for a short grace window only if a custody link, such as a worn band, is unbroken. Require a fresh 3D-face match on return and a full explicit check once the window lapses. Kill the session immediately, without waiting for decay, when a different face appears. Templates drift, behavioral ones most of all[106, 108], so adaptive updates should run only on samples that a strong modality co-verifies, which blocks an undetected impostor from poisoning the template (inference). Modality choice also has legal consequences: New York bars employers from requiring fingerprinting[92], which favors face, palm vein or iris as the mandatory factor there.

The known-spoof record shows why "internal feature" and "3D" marketing claims need testing. Gelatin fingers molded from latent prints fooled 11 fingerprint devices in 2002[91]. A printed photo plus a contact lens beat the Galaxy S8 iris scanner in 2017[100]. Certification therefore shows resistance to bounded attackers only, and Vault's threat model should assume bespoke artefacts such as silicone masks and custom wax hands (inference).

Injection matters more than any mask because it bypasses PAD entirely. The countermeasure that works is architectural. Apple's Touch ID channel is encrypted and authenticated with a per-sensor key. Face ID randomizes its sequence of 2D and depth captures and projects a device-specific pattern, which acts as a built-in challenge-response against replayed depth data[23]. ESS lets only the face camera write into hypervisor-protected memory[12]. The weakness of both is coverage: ESS does not support external cameras[12], and CyberArk warned that any system accepting third-party USB cameras as biometric sensors may be affected by frame injection[55]. Hardware indicators are not a substitute: the 2013 "iSeeYou" research disabled the hardware-interlocked camera LED on older MacBooks by reflashing camera firmware[119]. The notes therefore propose a procurement specification for Vault (inference). Every biometric sensor should carry iBeta Level 2 PAD or FIDO certification with IAPAR ≤7%. The false-match floor should be NIST's 1:10,000 per demographic group, set far lower in practice (for example 1e-6 for face), and the biometric should always be bound to a hardware authenticator. The camera should be integrated and firmware-locked, with frames signed to the secure element and an unpredictable illumination nonce, and USB and virtual cameras should be blocked by policy. Software injection-detection heuristics in the CEN/TS 18099 style then become defense in depth rather than the primary control.

06 · Duress & coercion

Signal explicitly, degrade silently, make coercion unprofitable

Signals and the quality of their evidence

Duress signals fall into two families. Explicit covert signals let the victim choose to signal, so they carry no "stress versus duress" ambiguity. Inferred signals try to read coercion from physiology, behavior or the environment. Only the first family has a long deployment record, and only the environmental and task-level parts of the second are likely to be both useful and lawful.

The deception-detection literature is the closest analog to "read a hidden mental state from physiology," and it is sobering. The National Academies found polygraph screening "an unacceptable choice" for employee security screening, and noted that no alternative such as facial expression, voice or brain activity "has yet been shown to outperform the polygraph"[27]. Physiology is also non-specific. Skin conductance tracks heat, dehydration and exertion as well as stress[133], and operators will be legitimately stressed exactly when Vault matters most: incident response, release deadlines, 3 a.m. pages.

Base rates sink inferred duress

The National Academies' polygraph example frames the arithmetic. In 10,000 employees containing 10 spies, a threshold that catches about 8 spies flags about 1,598 innocent people, and a threshold tuned to limit false alarms catches only 2[27]. Axelsson's analysis of intrusion detection makes the same point: for rare events, the false-alarm rate, not the detection rate, decides whether alerts are useful[28]. The notes work a Vault-specific illustration (their own arithmetic, not from a source). Fifty operators opening four sessions a day produce about 73,000 sessions a year. If genuine duress happens once a decade, a detector with 95% sensitivity and a 1% false-positive rate raises about 730 false alarms a year, and even 0.1% yields about 73. An inferred-stress detector therefore cannot drive alarms or police dispatch. At most it can add minutes of friction.

Response design: what pharmacies, vault locks and hardware wallets already know

The strongest lesson from ATMs, pharmacies, cash-in-transit, bank time locks and crypto self-custody is to make coercion unprofitable rather than detect it perfectly. Two-person control is the most battle-tested form. The US Air Force's nuclear two-person concept ensures "a lone individual cannot perform an incorrect act or unauthorized procedure" and designates no-lone zones[30]. Time delays are the second form. Electronic vault locks pair a 1–99 minute delay with a silent duress module (vendor)[134]. Police reported an 82% drop in Toronto pharmacy robberies in 2024 after time-delay safes[135]. Mandatory standards in British Columbia were reported to have cut pharmacy robberies 94%, and a Calgary detective states that time-delay safes "do not lead to an escalation of violence"[136]. These are uncontrolled before-and-after comparisons, and the "no escalation" claim is a police assertion, not a study. Removing the payoff is the third form. Belgium mandates banknote-staining systems for cash in transit[137], and Bitcoin covenant "vaults" add a delay window in which a recovery key can claw back a theft[138].

The second lesson is that the response must be invisible. The FTC's review quotes Illinois regulators saying the risk of harm "is greatly increased should they resist". It notes that visible alarms may raise the danger, and that only 26.4% of police responses to reported robberies arrive within five minutes[8]. Clark and Hengartner's analysis of panic passwords sets the formal requirement. The duress credential must be "indistinguishable" by the attacker, and any lockout must be "invariant to the type of the passwords being entered". A visible lockout invites "threatening retaliation". Attackers who can watch, iterate or force random choices among the victim's passwords will screen out inconsistent fake responses[29]. Coercion-resistant voting reaches the same answer with fake credentials indistinguishable from real ones[139]. Decoys must also be internally consistent: Windows, Word and Google Desktop each leaked evidence of a TrueCrypt hidden volume[140]. Coldcard's duress PIN shows the product form, a functioning decoy wallet where "nothing special is shown on the screen"[9]. GrapheneOS's irreversible duress wipe[122] is the wrong default for an operator held by an attacker who expects success.

Three supporting practices complete the design (proposal). Remote approvers need their own out-of-band duress channel and should be trained to appear to approve while escalating. When duress is suspected, someone should check on the operator's home and family, because coercion often happens there; that is the lesson of tiger kidnappings, where protocols put hostages' safe return first[49]. Duress actions must be rehearsed, simple and forgiving, because the FTC's concern that victims "fumble" emergency PINs is real[8]. Restoring full access should therefore need something the operator alone cannot do. Publicity may deter on its own. In one study, arrestees told they were being monitored by voice stress analysis lied less, even though the tool performed at chance[124].

07 · Succession & heirs

Heirs are a key ceremony, not a biometric

The products that pass control of high-value secrets to heirs fall into three patterns. Some split shares across trustees. Some use multisig, where the heir holds a dormant key and a custodian co-signs after a legal or time-delayed process. Others use social recovery, where guardians rotate the signing key after a delay the owner can veto. All of them pair cryptography with a waiting period and a human or legal attestation, and none relies on cryptography alone.

Coercion resistance in these systems comes from asymmetric delays: sensitive changes are slow and observable, while cancellation is fast. Apple's Stolen Device Protection requires biometric authentication, then a one-hour security delay, then biometric authentication again before a recovery contact or enrolled face can be changed away from familiar locations[59]. The withdrawn Bitcoin OP_VAULT proposal paired a hot trigger key with a cold recovery key that can claw funds back during a withdrawal delay, explicitly as a defense against coerced spends[152].

A proposed heir protocol for Vault

No standard exists for "biometric heirs," and this research found no product that binds owner-authorized biometric heirs to hardware. The following design is the notes' proposal, extrapolated from the patterns above. Designation should be an owner-signed delegation, generated and signed inside the owner's secure element, binding the heir's device-bound public key to a policy covering activation conditions, scope and expiry. The heir's template should be enrolled on the heir's own Vault and never exported. The owner's device holds only the heir's public key and policy, which keeps the design inside the on-device storage model that EU regulators favor[31, 153]. Adding an heir should be the most dangerous operation in the system. It should require live owner biometrics with liveness, live heir enrollment on the heir's own hardware, M-of-N guardian co-signatures, and a security delay of hours to days during which every guardian is notified and any one can veto. It should be allowed only in an enrolled secure facility. Revocation is the mirror image: instant for the owner, or quorum-only when the owner is unavailable. A monotonic policy-version counter in the secure element, plus re-wrapping of the succession key on every change, makes old wrapped copies useless. With this asymmetry, a coerced owner can be forced to start an addition but cannot complete it before others can intervene. Activation borrows Liana's model: every live owner session resets an inactivity counter, and the heir path becomes armable only after N days without owner presence and trustee attestation of death, incapacity or departure. A duress signal during "add heir" should make the ceremony appear to succeed while silently freezing it and alerting guardians, echoing SLIP-39's unverifiable decoy passphrase[143].

Device-only templates create one tension, which the notes flag. If a Vault is destroyed, the biometric cannot be restored from backup, so recovery must come from the cryptographic layer through re-enrollment authorized by quorum, never from template copies. This matches Apple's "never backed up" stance[153]. A second tension is this report's inference: RAND's SL5 list includes an "irrecoverable key policy"[1]. Vault should therefore support a mode with succession disabled for the highest tiers, in which recovery means re-keying the weights under a fresh quorum rather than inheriting access.

Why kinship and DNA fail as a gate

A "kinship heir" gate fails on every axis. It is slow: FBI-approved Rapid DNA booking systems take about 90 minutes (vendor release)[37]. It is not secret: in a 2024 University of New Hampshire study, one person's DNA transferred to a second person's hand in 50% of trials[38]. It is probabilistic for relatives: even at 40 STR loci, half-siblings showed 5–7% false inclusion or exclusion[39]. It is legally toxic: GINA bars US employers with 15 or more employees from requesting genetic information about an employee or an employee's family member[40], and genetic data is special-category data under GDPR Art. 9[154]. Succession must rest on explicit owner authorization, not biology.

Enterprise succession: successor operators and break-glass

In an AI lab, an heir is a successor operator, and the mature playbook already exists. It consists of M-of-N quorum on HSM administration, sealed break-glass credentials with alert-on-use, two-party authorization for weight access, and joiner–mover–leaver processes. Vault's contribution would be making each custodian's approval biometric-bound and non-delegable. A smartcard can be lent; a live face match on a dedicated terminal cannot (inference).

08 · Law & ethics

Verification is defensible, emotion inference is not

EU AI Act Art. 5(1)(f) and what it does to duress design

Art. 5(1)(f) prohibits placing on the market, putting into service or using AI systems "to infer emotions of a natural person in the areas of workplace and education institutions, except where the use … is intended … for medical or safety reasons"[159]. The prohibitions have applied since 2 February 2025, and the 2026 AI Omnibus did not delay them[159, 160]. The Commission's guidelines (C(2025) 5052 final, July 2025) are non-binding; only the Court of Justice can give an authoritative interpretation. They nonetheless set the regulatory reading, and that reading is wide[10]. The ban binds both providers and deployers (para 243). "Emotion recognition" covers inferring "emotions or intentions", the Recital 18 list is "not exhaustive", and the ban "should not be circumvented by referring to attitudes" (paras 244, 247–248). On stress the guidelines are explicit. "The general monitoring of stress levels at the workplace is not permitted under health or safety aspects", and an employer "would be prohibited from deploying AI-enabled devices … for measuring anxiety based on measured stress levels" unless elevated stress "would pose a specific danger", such as around dangerous machines or chemicals (paras 257, 263). In-scope biometric signals include keystrokes, heartbeats, ECG and EEG, and inferences from "key stroke …, facial expressions, body postures or movements" (para 251). "Workplace" includes contractors, trainees and candidates (para 254).

The safety exception is narrow. It applies "only in relation to the protection of life and health and not to protect other interests, for example property against theft or fraud" (para 258). Use under it must be "strictly necessary and proportionate", backed by a "prior written and motivated expert opinion", limited in time and scale, and barred from secondary use (paras 259–260). Workers must be consulted, the guidelines add that such systems "will require also consent", and Member States may ban workplace emotion recognition outright (para 264, footnote 167)[10]. Law-firm commentary reads the exception as excluding routine monitoring "reframed as safety"[161]. The consequence for Vault is direct. Duress inference justified as protecting model weights protects property, so the exception very likely does not apply. Duress inference framed as protecting the operator's life is arguable but untested, and a regulator would look at the marketed purpose (inference).

Verification sits outside the AI Act's riskiest categories

Vault's identity function is low-risk under the AI Act; its duress function is where the hazard lies. Biometric verification against a template "stored on a device" is outside the remote-biometric-identification ban. Confirming identity "for the sole purpose of … unlocking a device, or having security access to premises" is excluded from "remote" (guidelines paras 303–305)[10], and Annex III(1)(a) excludes sole-purpose verification from high-risk identification[162]. One uncertainty remains: no guidance addresses continuous, passive re-verification, which involves less active participation than a one-time unlock (inference). The 2026 AI Omnibus moved Annex III high-risk obligations to 2 December 2027[160]. Whether the national-security exclusion in Art. 2(3) could cover a lab's classified work was not researched.

GDPR: necessity, on-device templates and a pending exception

Biometric data processed to uniquely identify someone is special-category data under GDPR Art. 9. Member States may add conditions, and high-risk processing needs a DPIA under Art. 35[154]. Employee consent is presumptively invalid because of the power imbalance[163]. Employers therefore need a national legal basis and demonstrated necessity. The Netherlands' implementing act permits biometrics "necessary for authentication or security purposes", and its explanatory memorandum contrasts a nuclear power plant (likely justified) with a car garage (unlikely)[164]. The Dutch regulator fined a company €725,000 in 2020 for fingerprint time clocks[165], and Belgium fined an employer €45,000 in 2024 for consent-based fingerprint time tracking[166]. France's CNIL limits workplace biometrics to access control to premises, devices and applications, rejects them where a badge would do, prefers templates under the person's exclusive control ("Type 1"), and makes a DPIA mandatory[32]. The EDPB's Opinion 11/2024 found templates held on the individual's own device "in principle compatible" with GDPR, and centralized operator-controlled templates "cannot be compatible" with Art. 25[31]. A Commission proposal would add an Art. 9(2)(l) exception for verification where the biometric data and means "remain under the data subject's sole control". The EDPB and EDPS welcomed it in Joint Opinion 2/2026, but it was still a proposal at last check[167]. Every enforcement case found involved time and attendance, so Vault should never be positioned or configured as an attendance or productivity tool (inference). A lab protecting model weights sits near the nuclear-plant end of the Dutch test, but continuous monitoring needs its own necessity case. The defensible configuration verifies at session start and at sensitive actions, with continuous checks running as on-device presence that keeps no stored history (inference).

The US has no general ban on workplace emotion recognition. Exposure comes from biometric-privacy statutes, notice laws and a few modality-specific rules.

Ethics: power, function creep and the operator's safety

Three ethical risks deserve design attention. The first is power. Workplace-law commentators argue that even when employers cannot read templates, the imbalance that makes employee consent invalid remains, and that on-device exceptions could legitimize systematic employer biometrics[180]. Function creep is the second. A continuous presence log is personal data and can become productivity surveillance, which Colorado expressly resists[175], so logs should be minimal, local and limited to security events (inference). The third is the operator's physical safety, which should be an explicit design objective. Visible lockouts and wipes can get a coerced person hurt[8, 29], and a published policy that no single person can release weights quickly protects operators the way "staff cannot open this safe" signage protects pharmacists (inference). Frontier-AI laws push labs toward more insider monitoring, while the AI Act and GDPR push against biometric inference about employees. The notes conclude (inference) that the two meet in one design: verification, explicit duress signals and multi-party approval, without affect inference.

09 · Market & competitors

A dozen buyers, rising mandates and an empty intersection

Buyers and who owns the problem

The core buyer set is very small. Epoch AI counted more than 30 publicly announced models above 10^25 FLOP from 12 developers as of June 2025[181], and CAISI's pre-deployment testing agreements covered five frontier labs in May 2026[182]. The notes estimate (inference) about 5–12 tier-one frontier developers. Around them sits a second ring of hyperscalers, neoclouds and inference providers holding partner weights, and a third ring of government AI and intelligence data centers, which the 2025 US AI Action Plan prioritizes[183] — fewer than 100 organizations in 2026 by the notes' rough count. Ownership sits with the CISO and security engineering. Anthropic's CISO was quoted in 2023 saying he spends "almost half of my time" protecting "that one file", as cited in House testimony[184]. Sales will be few, large and high-touch.

Compliance hooks create demand but do not mandate biometrics

Weight-security obligations now exist in law and quasi-law. California's SB 53 requires large frontier developers to publish frameworks describing "cybersecurity practices to secure unreleased model weights from unauthorized modification or transfer by internal or external parties". It treats weight exfiltration as a critical safety incident, with civil penalties up to $1M per violation (law-firm summary)[185]. New York's amended RAISE Act takes effect on 1 January 2027[186]. Appendix 4 of the EU GPAI Code of Practice (as reproduced by an unofficial mirror of the final text) calls for "restrictions on device and account sharing", MFA, zero trust, authorization reviews "at least every six months", limits on "the number of people with non-hardened interface-access", background checks and "periodic personnel integrity testing"[187]. RAND's August 2026 SL3 follow-up proposes 262 controls adapted from NIST SP 800-53 (search listing; full text not read)[188]. OpenAI aligns its security controls with ISO 27001, SOC 2, NIST SP 800-53 and FedRAMP[3]. None of these mandates biometrics. Buyers will compare Vault with FIDO2 keys plus two-person rules, so Vault's marginal value must be non-delegability (cards can be lent, faces cannot) and presence continuity (inference).

Lab frameworks leave the human unverified

Anthropic's ASL-3 protections (May 2025) include "more than 100 different security controls", "two-party authorization for model weight access" and egress bandwidth controls, but place sophisticated insider risk "out of the scope"[2]. Its RSP v3.0 points to security "roughly in line with RAND SL4" in industry-wide recommendations for higher capability thresholds, and describes "moonshot R&D for security" projects[44]. OpenAI's Preparedness Framework v2 requires "hardware-backed security", strong MFA, managed devices and "multi-person approval" for critical infrastructure changes[3]. Secondary coverage reports that OpenAI paused work on a model in August 2026 and is rewriting its framework after an evaluation-related breach; OpenAI's own posts were not retrieved[189]. Also per secondary coverage, Google DeepMind's FSF v3.1 adds "ID verification for staff with sensitive access"[190], and Meta limits protections to what is "technically feasible and commercially available"[191]. The Frontier Model Forum recommends phishing-resistant MFA, no "static or persistent credentials" and quorum operations[11]. The notes infer that Anthropic is the most likely early design partner, given its insider exclusion and its "moonshot R&D" language. They see Google DeepMind's ID-verification language as a second hook, OpenAI's rewrite as a time-bounded window, and Meta's "commercially available" caveat as a signal that Meta would buy rather than build.

Market size and go-to-market

Generic privileged access management is sizeable but not Vault's market. Third-party estimates for 2025 range from about $2.5B to $4.5B, with growth rates of 15–24%, and the estimates disagree widely[201, 202]. Vault's niche is a wedge. The closest comparable is Irregular (formerly Pattern Labs), which raised $80M in September 2025 led by Sequoia and Redpoint, selling security work to a handful of frontier labs; its co-founder co-authored the RAND weights report[203]. The notes propose this path (inference). Start with a design partnership with one lab's security engineering team. Map Vault to RAND SL3/SL4 controls, EU Appendix 4 and SB 53 framework language. Obtain SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001, and later FedRAMP or CMMC for government and data-center buyers. Pass a physical red-team validation. The biggest competitive threats are Teleport, Palo Alto/CyberArk and labs' in-house teams. Confidential-computing vendors are natural partners, with Vault deciding who may trigger key release and the vendor deciding where it happens. The nonprofit Security Level 5 convenes labs, data-center operators and chip providers, which is a natural standards venue[204].

Platform choice and the continuity compromise

The hardest v0 decision is where continuous matching runs. Apple-silicon Macs with a paired Touch ID peripheral and Secure Enclave keys are the most mature commodity option for per-operation biometric-gated signing[23, 5]. On Windows, ESS fingerprint beats ESS face for a desk terminal because ESS face does not support external cameras[12]. Neither platform exposes continuous secure-element face re-verification, and secure camera paths on Linux, the likely environment of lab infrastructure, were not found in this research. The notes therefore imply an honest v0 compromise (inference). "Continuous" in v0 means a hardware-gated biometric signature on every privileged action, which satisfies RAND's "every interaction or ~60 seconds" benchmark[1], plus passive attested face presence that can only lower trust. True secure-element-continuous face matching is a Phase 2 question that may require a custom sensor-plus-secure-element module.

Proposed Phase 2 prototype scope

The Phase 2 prototype should prove the hybrid end to end against mock weights, with acceptance criteria drawn from the evidence. The targets below are proposals, not established benchmarks.

Out of scope for Phase 2: physiological or voice duress inference, DNA or kinship gates, keys derived from biometrics, unsupervised home use, and SL5 facility controls.

The evidence changes what "biometric access control" should mean for frontier AI. The hard part is not recognizing a face; on cooperative images, leading matchers already operate well inside NIST's requirements. The hard part is turning recognition into a short-lived cryptographic fact that the system holding the weights can rely on, and arranging people, delays and decoys so that a recognized but coerced operator cannot deliver what an attacker wants. That reframing also resolves the apparent conflict between frontier-AI security mandates and EU worker protection: a Vault that verifies identity, accepts explicit signals and requires quorum meets both, while one that infers emotions satisfies neither the statistics nor the law.
